It's not a talent problem.

That's the most common diagnosis among introverts, and the most wrong.

Selling and performing are two very different things. Performing means projecting an image: confidence, charisma, the person who never has a doubt. If you're an introvert, that's exhausting and fake. Selling is offering help, and saying so clearly.

Playing small looks careful, but it's what costs you the most: the client leaves without understanding what you do, you get paid below your value, and the character wears you out. This guide replaces the sales show with selling that fits you.

The 5-move method

None of them relies on charisma. All of them rely on clarity and listening. One conversation at a time, you sell in a way that fits you.

01

The clear sentence

Answer "what do you do?" without shrinking, with one sentence prepared in advance.

02

The conversation, not the pitch

Ask questions instead of reciting. Selling becomes an exchange, not a performance.

03

Your three channels

Pick three places to be findable and stick to them. Consistency beats scattering.

04

Quiet presence

Stay available without ever harassing. All the space between silence and pushing.

05

The honest follow-up

Come back without forcing, to recover a conversation that stayed open with no reply.
