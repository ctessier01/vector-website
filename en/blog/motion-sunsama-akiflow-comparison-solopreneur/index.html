Motion vs Sunsama vs Akiflow: Which Planning Tool Actually Works for Solopreneurs?

5 min read

You tried Motion for three weeks. It scheduled everything on its own, reshuffled your tasks, filled your calendar for you. Then one morning you realized you no longer understood why a given task was there, at that exact hour. You stopped trusting your own calendar. You gave up.

If that sounds familiar, you're not alone. The market for AI planning tools has exploded, and many solopreneurs try them and abandon them within the first few weeks. And the reason is almost always the same: a mismatch between the tool and how they actually work.

Let's honestly compare the three best known: Motion, Sunsama, and Akiflow. Each has real strengths and real limits. The goal isn't to crown a universal winner, but to help you see which fits your profile, and why none of them was designed specifically for you.

  • Motion, which schedules everything for you, automatically, and recalculates your week.
  • Sunsama, a manual planning ritual, every morning.
  • Akiflow, which centralizes your scattered tasks in one place.

Motion: the power of automatic, and its price

Motion bets everything on automation. You enter your tasks, their deadlines and estimated durations, and the AI builds your calendar for you, then recalculates it the moment something unexpected lands. On paper, it's the dream: you no longer plan, you just execute what's on screen.

Its strength is real. For someone who hates planning and juggles many shifting tasks, Motion lifts an enormous mental load. The automatic rescheduling spares you from rebuilding everything by hand each time the day goes sideways, which happens often when you're alone at the controls.

Its limit is the exact flip side of its strength. The automation is a black box. You don't always know why it placed a task at a given time, and when you disagree, taking back control is frustrating. Many users describe the same drift: by not understanding the calendar's decisions, they stop believing it. Add a premium price, and staying committed gets hard.

To sum up: Motion is built for someone who wants zero planning and is willing to trust a machine. If you need to understand and keep control of your calendar, that's exactly where the relationship tends to break down.

A calendar you don't understand isn't a calendar. It's a black box you obey.

Sunsama: the ritual that calms, and that weighs

Sunsama takes the complete opposite stance. No magic automation, but a ritual: every morning, you plan your day by hand, task by task, pulling what matters from your various tools. The app forces you to be intentional, to choose instead of just react.

That's its great strength. The daily ritual creates calm and clarity. You start each day knowing exactly what you'll do, and why. For someone who feels swamped the moment they open their laptop, that gentle discipline is worth a lot, it puts order in your head before it puts order in your calendar.

But the ritual has a cost, and that's its limit. Planning by hand every morning takes time, every single day, no exceptions. And on busy weeks, that ritual is exactly the first thing you sacrifice, so the tool loses its value precisely when you'd need it most. With, on top, a subscription among the priciest in its category.

So Sunsama suits anyone with the time and the taste for a daily appointment with their day. If your mornings are already under pressure, the ritual risks becoming one more chore rather than a refuge.

Akiflow: the command center, and its learning curve

Akiflow plays a third card: centralization. It gathers in one place the tasks scattered across your tools, your calendar and your email, and lets you drag them into your calendar as time blocks, all driven by keyboard, very fast.

Its strength is killing the scatter. If your real problem is having tasks in ten different places, Akiflow becomes a single command center, fast and formidably efficient for anyone who likes working from the keyboard.

Its limit is the climb to get there. All that power is paid for in complexity: shortcuts to memorize, integrations to wire up, configuration to fine-tune. Before it pays off, the tool demands a real setup investment, and many quit before reaching it. At heart, Akiflow excels at gathering and slotting your tasks, far less at deciding with you what truly matters this week. It organizes brilliantly, but it will not tell you where to start, and on a packed week that gap is exactly what hurts.

Akiflow is ideal for technical profiles who enjoy optimizing their system. If what you mainly want is a tool that tells you what to do first, you may be disappointed: it organizes remarkably well, but it doesn't prioritize for you.
